# Honor the Venue

Conference rules mutate. Templates change, page limits move, anonymity policies acquire exceptions, and a checklist someone remembers from two years ago becomes a desk-rejection generator. The current venue instructions are the brief.

## 1. Read the Current Official Rules

**Never substitute memory for this year's instructions.**

- Verify the named venue, track, submission year, and phase: initial submission, rebuttal, revision, artifact evaluation, or camera-ready.
- Use current official author instructions, templates, calls for papers, and policy pages as the source of truth.
- Treat third-party guides and prior-year papers as examples, not authority.

Failure signal: a rule is justified with “this conference usually...”

## 2. Freeze the Constraint Set

**Record the rules that can invalidate the submission.**

Capture the constraints that apply, including when relevant:

- page and reference limits,
- template and formatting requirements,
- anonymity and self-citation rules,
- supplementary-material policy,
- artifact and reproducibility requirements,
- ethics or broader-impact statements,
- data/code disclosure rules,
- LLM-use disclosure,
- rebuttal format and word limits.

Record when they were checked so stale requirements are visible.

## 3. Let Constraints Shape the Paper Early

**Do not write a twelve-page paper and discover an eight-page limit at the end.**

- Allocate space around the actual venue budget.
- Put material in appendices or supplements only when the rules allow reviewers to rely on it.
- Design figures, references, and section depth with the final format in mind rather than shrinking everything into illegibility on deadline night.

## 4. Protect Anonymity and Policy Compliance

**Metadata can betray what prose hides.**

- Check author fields, acknowledgements, repository links, artifact URLs, PDF metadata, filenames, self-citation language, and supplementary files.
- Distinguish double-blind constraints from requirements that still demand disclosure to chairs or through separate forms.
- Never remove required ethics, conflict, or disclosure information merely to make the manuscript cleaner.

## 5. Re-Validate at Every Submission Phase

**Camera-ready is not just anonymous submission with the names restored.**

- Re-check the rules when moving from submission to rebuttal, revision, artifact evaluation, or camera-ready.
- Validate the final rendered artifact, not merely the source files.
- Report any rule you could not verify rather than silently assuming continuity.

## The final test

Every submission-critical choice can be traced to a current venue rule for the correct year and phase, and the final rendered package has been checked against that frozen constraint set.
