# Answer the Reviewers

A rebuttal is not a courtroom speech about why the reviewer should have understood you. It is issue resolution under a word limit: identify the concern, answer it directly, show evidence, and state exactly what changes.

## 1. Extract the Concern Beneath the Wording

**Respond to the technical issue, not the reviewer's tone.**

- Rewrite each comment internally as the question or risk it raises.
- Split compound comments so distinct issues receive distinct answers.
- If the review is based on a misunderstanding, first ask what in the manuscript allowed that misunderstanding to happen.

Failure signal: the response spends more words defending intent than addressing the concern.

## 2. Answer First, Explain Second

**The reviewer should know your position in the first sentence.**

- Start with the direct answer, result, correction, or concession.
- Follow with the minimum evidence needed to justify it.
- Quote or restate only enough of the comment to orient the response; do not spend the word budget reproducing the review.

## 3. Change the Paper When the Review Exposes a Real Gap

**Winning the argument is less useful than fixing the artifact.**

- If a concern reveals unclear scope, missing evidence, weak positioning, or an actual error, revise the manuscript and say where.
- When new experiments are needed, state what was run, why it addresses the concern, and the result without overselling it.
- If the requested experiment is infeasible or would not test the stated concern, explain that precisely and offer the closest valid evidence or narrow the claim.

## 4. Make Every Commitment Traceable

**Promises in rebuttal become obligations in revision.**

Track each material item as:

`Reviewer/comment → response → evidence → manuscript change → location → status`

Do not promise an experiment, analysis, release, or rewrite that will silently disappear after the response period.

## 5. Keep the Whole Response Consistent

**Reviewers are separate; the paper is not.**

- Do not give incompatible explanations to different reviewers.
- Reuse evidence where concerns overlap, but tailor the answer to the actual issue.
- Preserve one claim boundary across rebuttal and manuscript; a weakened claim in one response cannot remain inflated elsewhere.

## The final test

A meta-reviewer can take any concern and trace it to a direct answer, supporting evidence, and either a concrete revision or an explicit reason the paper should not change.
